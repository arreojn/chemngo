Lessons

1

Combination (Synthesis)

Two or more substances join to form one product.

  • Definition: A combination reaction joins two or more reactants into one product.
  • Pattern: A + B → AB
  • Example: Hydrogen + oxygen forms water.
  • Everyday use: It helps create compounds like salts, rust, and many materials we use daily.
2

Decomposition Reaction

One compound breaks apart into simpler substances.

  • Definition: One compound breaks into simpler substances.
  • Pattern: AB → A + B
  • How it happens: Heat, electricity, or chemical change can trigger it.
  • Example: Water can split into hydrogen and oxygen gases.
3

Single Displacement

One element replaces another in a compound.

  • Definition: One element replaces another in a compound.
  • Pattern: A + BC → AC + B
  • Example: Zinc can replace copper in copper sulfate solution.
  • Where it appears: Metals, batteries, and industrial reactions often use this pattern.
4

Double Displacement

Ions exchange between two compounds to form new products.

  • Definition: Two compounds swap ions to form two new compounds.
  • Pattern: AB + CD → AD + CB
  • Common result: A solid precipitate, gas, or water can form.
  • Example: Silver nitrate + sodium chloride → silver chloride + sodium nitrate.
5

Combustion

A fuel reacts with oxygen and releases energy as heat and light.

  • Definition: A fuel reacts quickly with oxygen and gives off heat and light.
  • Pattern: Fuel + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
  • Example: Burning wood, gasoline, or natural gas.
  • Important note: It provides energy for cooking, heating, and engines.
