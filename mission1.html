What is Chemistry in Everyday Life?

Chemistry explains how matter changes in cooking, rusting, digestion, and even in the air around us.

Chemistry is the study of matter and the changes it undergoes. It explains what happens when food cooks, when metals rust, and when cells break down nutrients in your body. Everyday processes such as baking bread, boiling water, and breathing all involve chemical changes.

2

Physical Change vs Chemical Change

A physical change alters appearance, while a chemical change creates a new substance with different properties.

A physical change changes the form or appearance of a substance without creating a new one. Examples include melting ice, crushing a can, and dissolving sugar in water. A chemical change forms a new substance with different properties, such as rusting iron, burning paper, or baking a cake.

3

Signs of a Chemical Reaction

Gas production, color change, temperature change, and precipitate formation can signal a chemical reaction.

Scientists look for clues such as bubbles, a color change, heat or cold, sound, or a solid appearing in a liquid. These signs suggest that a chemical reaction is taking place and new substances are being formed. Rusting, digestion, and combustion are all examples of such reactions.
